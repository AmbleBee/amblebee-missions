Privacy

Last updated November 19, 2026

At AmbleBee, we think you should know what happens to your information, why we collect it, and how long we keep it.

The short version

  • No ads, and no account needed to explore missions.
  • Most of what you do here stays in your own browser.
  • We count visits on our own server, with no cookies and no IP addresses kept.
  • Google Analytics only runs if you say yes.
  • A profile is optional, and your personal notes are locked so even we can't read them.

We try to collect only what has a clear purpose. When something is optional, we want you to know what you're agreeing to. When we no longer need information, we don't keep it.

Here's how that works.

What stays on your device

A lot of AmbleBee works entirely in your browser, so the information it uses is saved there and never sent to us.

  • Your ZIP code or city helps you find nearby missions. It's forgotten when you close the tab, unless you choose "Remember on this device." Then it stays in your browser until you press Clear. The lookup happens in your browser, not on our server.
  • Your location is only used if you press "Use my location" and allow it. It stays in the open tab, is used to work out distances and show the nearest ZIP code, and is never saved or sent to us.
  • Your preferences: miles or kilometers, the name, theme and sign-off you last used on a card, and whether opening a mission should include ones you've done.
  • Whether you've been here before: a count of pages you've opened and whether you've opened a mission, so returning visitors don't see the "New to AmbleBee?" introduction again.
  • A card you're writing stays in that browser tab until the tab closes, so it isn't lost by accident.
  • Your Google Analytics answer (yes or no), so we don't keep asking.
  • A form you're filling in (a suggestion, report or message) is saved as you type, so a failed send never loses your work. It's cleared once it's sent.
  • Your submissions: the private status links you get, so you can find them again. "Forget" removes one.
  • Your missions: the ones you mark done, the dates, your ratings, your personal notes, missions you've marked "Not for us," and the home state and anniversary day you set for stamps, so your profile page can show them. Your stamps are worked out from this on your device. If you add a recovery email to a profile, your browser also keeps it, only to fill in our forms for you.
  • Your plans: missions you add to your calendar, with the day and any start time, so My plans can show them and ask whether you went.

Unless you start a free profile (below), none of your mission history is sent to us. A backup code or file you make holds the same information, so keep it somewhere private.

Clearing this site's data in your browser removes all of it.

Why we keep so much on your device

Your location helps you find nearby adventures, but we don't need to know where you live. Your mission history helps you remember what you've done, but we don't need a copy of it to show you your progress.

The tradeoff is that clearing your browser can erase your progress. That's why we offer backups, and an optional profile for anyone who wants their missions on more than one device.

Cards and shared links

When you seal a card, the names and message are packed into the link itself instead of being stored on a server. They're hidden, but not encrypted, so anyone who has the link could read them. Treat a card link like a text message, and don't put anything in it you'd want kept secret.

We do it this way so we don't need a database of personal messages just to let someone open a card.

Links to a list of missions

Your filters and search words go in the page address, so you can share a list or refresh the page without losing it. Your ZIP code, city and location are never included.

Adding a mission to your calendar

"Add to calendar" sends nothing anywhere until you pick a calendar. If you pick Google Calendar or Outlook, that one event (the mission's name, the day and time, a short description, the place and a link back to the mission) goes to Google or Microsoft so you can save it, and their privacy policy applies. "Apple and other calendars" makes a small file on your device instead, which isn't sent anywhere.

Counting visits (our own, no cookies)

We count visits on a small server we run ourselves, so we can tell whether AmbleBee is working without using cookies or keeping records of who visited.

What we count:

  • Each page opened, without the search words or filters in its address.
  • The site you came from, like Google or Pinterest, never the full link.
  • Whether you're on a phone, tablet or computer.
  • A few actions by name: opening a mission, adding one to a calendar, starting a vote.

What we don't keep: no cookies, nothing saved in your browser, and no IP addresses. To count each visitor once a day, our server mixes your IP address and browser details with a secret that changes every day, and keeps only the scrambled result. Both the result and the secret are deleted after two days, so it can't be turned back into your address, and the next day you're counted as someone new.

What we keep for good: only daily totals, like "184 visitors on November 19." Nothing in them points to a person.

What we don't count: bots, and browsers that send the Global Privacy Control signal. None of this is sent to any other company.

Why we count visits ourselves

Without some numbers, we couldn't tell whether people are finding AmbleBee, which pages get used, or whether a new feature works. Running the count ourselves means we decide exactly what's collected and how long it lasts. We don't need to know who visited to know whether a page is useful, which is why the daily scrambled codes are deleted and only totals are kept.

Visitor stats (Google Analytics, optional)

On top of our own count, we ask whether you're OK with Google Analytics. It's off unless you say yes. Until you do, Google Analytics isn't loaded at all, and nothing about your visit goes to Google from us.

Saying no costs you nothing: every mission and feature works the same either way.

If you say yes, Google Analytics sets a cookie so it can tell a returning visitor from a new one, and records:

  • The pages you open.
  • A few actions by name: opening a mission, sealing a card, choosing date or adventure, and opening or sending one of our forms.
  • Roughly where you are (city or region), worked out by Google from your connection.
  • Your type of device and browser.
  • Which site sent you here.

We only send the page itself. Your ZIP code, city, location, search words, filters and the contents of any card are never sent.

Google says Google Analytics 4 doesn't log or store IP addresses. Google signals and ad personalization are turned off, and the detailed data is kept for two months, the shortest option. We only see totals and charts, never who you are, and nothing is used for ads. Google's privacy policy applies to what it collects.

Why we use both

Our own count tells us whether the site is working. Google Analytics shows us broader patterns: which packs people open, whether more of you are out on dates or adventures, and roughly which states and cities the missions are used in. That's how we decide where to write the next pack, which missions need a second look, and where to spend our limited time.

We know not everyone wants Google involved in their browsing, and that's fair. That's why it's your choice. And if you'd rather just tell us what you think, we'd love that even more: our contact page is always open.

Your choice

You can change your mind any time. Saying no stops Google Analytics and removes its cookies from this browser. This choice is only about Google Analytics. Our own count above uses no cookies and keeps nothing that identifies you.

Turn on JavaScript to see or change your choice.

Your profile (optional)

You can use AmbleBee without a profile. If you'd like your missions kept safe when you switch devices or clear your browser, you can start a free one, stored on a small server we run ourselves.

How it works

  • No username, no password. Your browser makes a long random code. Your profile link holds it after the # sign, which browsers never send to a server. Our server keeps only a scrambled fingerprint of the code, so it can't open your profile on its own.
  • Anyone with your profile link can open your profile. Keep the link private, like a key. A PIN (below) adds a second lock on new devices.

What your profile keeps

  • The missions you mark done, the dates, and whether each was a date.
  • Your thumbs up or down.
  • Missions you've marked "Not for us."
  • Your home state and anniversary day for stamps.
  • Your plans, and whether a mission you marked done was planned ahead.
  • Your display name, if you set one.
  • Your personal notes, locked.

That's all.

Display name (optional): a first name or nickname you choose. It fills in your name when you vote with friends and labels the messages you send us. It isn't shown anywhere else.

PIN (optional): asked when your profile is opened on a new device. We keep only a scrambled copy, never the PIN itself. We also keep a list of the devices signed in: a random code for each (scrambled), a rough name like "Firefox on Linux," and when it was first and last seen. Signing out, or "Sign out other devices," removes them, and a device not seen for 90 days is removed on its own.

Your messages: anything you send through the contact or suggestion forms while signed in is linked to your profile, so we can see your earlier messages and you can read our replies under Messages. Anyone with your profile link can read them too, unless you've set a PIN and they're on a new device. Closed conversations leave Messages 90 days after closing. If you remove your profile, your messages are unlinked from it and handled like any other message (see Forms, below).

Your notes are locked with your profile link before they leave your device, so our server only holds scrambled text we can't read. Your link, or your recovery email (below), unlocks them.

What we can see

For each profile, under a random label that never says who you are, we can see:

  • Which missions it marked done, and its ratings.
  • Its plans.
  • The votes it started.
  • The feedback it sent.
  • When it was started and last used.
  • How many devices are signed in.

We never see which pages you look at, and never what your notes say. Your ratings aren't shown publicly, and nothing is shared or sold.

Recovery email (optional)

We use it once, to send your profile link and recovery code. After that we keep only a scrambled fingerprint of the address, never the address itself, so we can match it if you ask for a new link. Opening a new link replaces the old one, which stops working. Your notes also get a second lock that only your recovery email opens: with a new link, you type the email once to unlock them.

We'll never email you for anything else. Without a recovery email, your profile link is the only way back in.

How long we keep it

  • A profile with nothing marked done is removed after 90 days without a visit.
  • A profile with missions is kept until you remove it. Settings → Remove account deletes it from our server and your browser.
  • Nightly backups hold a copy a little longer, until they're replaced by newer ones.

We don't store your IP address. To stop abuse, the server briefly counts requests from each connection, only in memory, and the count is gone within an hour.

Why profiles work this way

We didn't want anyone to have to sign up just to start exploring. A profile is there when you want a backup or more than one device, not as a gate.

We also keep your notes apart from what we need to run the site. Knowing that a mission was done helps us see which adventures people enjoy. Reading your memories doesn't, so your notes are locked before they ever reach us.

Votes with friends

  • What a vote keeps: its name, the day it's for, the missions in it, and each ballot: which missions were picked, and a name the voter chose to add (optional). Nobody sees who picked what.
  • One vote each: your browser keeps a random code so you're counted once, and our server keeps only a scrambled version of it, different for every vote. If you're signed in, your ballot is tied to your profile instead, so your phone and computer count once. That link is used only for your own plans and stamps.
  • No IP addresses. To stop one network flooding a vote, the server keeps a scrambled fingerprint of it while voting is open, and deletes it when voting ends.
  • Your display name, if you set one, fills in your name on votes and shows as "Started by" on votes you start. You can change it for any vote.
  • How long: a vote is deleted 30 days after it ends, when ten newer ones from the same person push it out, or when the person who started it deletes it. The winner stays in each voter's own plans.

A vote needs to remember its options and ballots long enough to reach a decision, but not forever, so old ballots are cleared once they've done their job.

Feedback on a mission

  • Feedback is written to us. After a thumbs up or down on a mission you've marked done, you can tell us what you liked or what didn't work. The AmbleBee team reads it. It's kept with the mission, your thumbs up or down, and the date.
  • Your personal notes are different. They're locked to your profile and we never see them. Nothing from a note is ever sent as feedback.
  • Using it: only if you say yes when we ask (nothing is pre-chosen), we may quote it as "an AmbleBee user," never with your name, or pass a problem on to the place so they can fix it.
  • How long: feedback sent while signed in is linked to your profile, and you can delete it any time in Settings. Removing your profile deletes it too. Feedback sent without a profile can't be tied back to you, so it's kept. Please leave out names and contact details.

A thumbs up tells us someone liked a mission, but not why. Written feedback is how we find confusing steps, outdated details or places that aren't what they used to be, and the people who actually did the mission are the best judges of that.

AmbleBee polls

Now and then we ask visitors to vote on decisions about AmbleBee.

  • What a poll keeps: each ballot's pick (or ranking), and what you wrote in "Other," if you chose it. No names. Only AmbleBee sees "Other" answers; they're never shown publicly.
  • One vote each works the same way as votes with friends: a scrambled code from your browser, or your profile if you're signed in. We also note whether a ballot came from a profile, and whether that profile has missions marked done, so we can spot a flooded poll. Results only ever show totals.
  • No IP addresses. The scrambled network fingerprint is deleted when the poll closes.
  • Had My Say: if you're signed in, your profile keeps the day you first voted in a poll, for the stamp. Not which poll, and not how you voted.
  • How long: polls and their results stay, so the decisions they helped make stay explained. If you remove your profile, your ballots stay in the count but are no longer tied to anything.

Forms

"Suggest a mission," "Report a problem" and the contact form go to a small server we run ourselves, not to Google. They travel encrypted the whole way.

  • What we keep is what you type. Name and email are optional on suggestions and reports. Names are kept as a first name and last initial, like "Jane D." The contact form needs an email so we can reply.
  • Your email is only used to write to you about what you sent, like when your suggestion goes live. We never use it for marketing, and we never share it.
  • Your name appears on a mission only if you tick "Credit me."
  • Sent while signed in, a message or suggestion is linked to your profile's random label (never anything that says who you are), so it shows in your Messages with our replies.
  • Status links are private. Anyone with the link can see where that one submission is at, but never your name or email. Treat it like a text message.
  • If our server is down, the Suggest and Report pages offer a backup Google Form instead. What you enter there goes to Google and to us, Google's privacy policy applies, and there's no status link.
  • No IP addresses. To stop spam, the server briefly counts how often each connection sends a form, only in memory, and the count is gone within an hour.
  • How long: 90 days after your suggestion goes live or is declined, or your report is settled, your name and email are replaced with "AmbleBee user." Contact messages and the replies back and forth are deleted 90 days after we've dealt with them. Nightly backups hold a copy a little longer, until they're replaced. Your name stays on a mission only if you asked to be credited. To have something removed sooner, just ask.

Please don't send sensitive personal details.

We collect contact details only when they have a job to do: replying to you, asking a question, or telling you your suggestion went live. The 90 days give us time to follow up or reopen something, and after that there's no reason to keep your details.

Hosting and other sites

The site is hosted on GitHub Pages. GitHub may log visitors' IP addresses for security, as its own privacy policy describes. Our fonts are hosted with the site, so loading a page doesn't contact Google unless you've said yes to Google Analytics.

Links to maps, places and other resources take you to sites with their own privacy policies.

Children

Missions can be fun with kids, but the site is meant for grown-ups to use. Children shouldn't send us their personal details through the forms. If you think a child has, please tell us and we'll delete it.

Changes

If this page changes, the date at the top changes too. The full history of every change is public on GitHub, so you never have to take our word for what it used to say.

A final word

We aren't claiming to collect nothing. Running and improving a website takes some information, and a few optional features take more. What we can promise is to collect only what has a purpose, explain why, and give you a real choice wherever we can.

Thank you for trusting us with a small part of your adventures.
